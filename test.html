FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Understand the model.

What makes patch management autonomous rather than simply automated?

Fixed automation follows predefined steps. Autonomous Patch Management evaluates changing risk, readiness, ownership and execution context to advance, pause or recover within approved guardrails.

Does autonomous patching remove human approval?

No. Accountable people retain authority where policy requires approval or override. The system handles eligible routine work and brings exceptions forward for judgment.

Why is Patch Governance a separate product?

Governance defines policy, decision rights, deadlines, triage and evidence expectations. Keeping it separate from execution preserves accountability and makes the separation of duties explicit.

How does the lifecycle close?

Post-implementation validation confirms the outcome, then the complete lifecycle record returns to Patch Governance for compliance reporting, SLA review and audit evidence.

GETTING STARTED

Platform overview

RBC Cloud Governance and Compliance organizes operational risk capabilities into domains. Each domain uses shared patterns for policy, accountable decisions, workflow continuity and evidence while keeping domain-specific execution separate.

Current domains

  • Patch Management: autonomous remediation with independent governance.
  • Configuration Management: unified compliance reporting, data quality and coverage.
Design principle: governance authorizes and constrains work; orchestration carries out the permitted path.

AUTONOMOUS PATCH MANAGEMENT

Patch lifecycle

RBC Cloud Autonomous Patch Management operates a closed discover → approve → rollout & deploy → verify loop, expanded into the ten stages delivery teams use in practice. This Patch Management workflow orchestrates unique, platform-specific Ansible playbooks owned by each platform team. Routine work advances inside policy; risky or ambiguous work pauses for human review.

  1. Discovery: use agentic patch intelligence to actively find new patches across approved vendor and platform sources, correlate release and CVE evidence, and map the affected scope to Asset Inventory.
  2. Notification: resolve and engage accountable owners.
  3. Approval: auto-advance eligible work or pause for human decision and override.
  4. Testing: execute the platform team's unique Ansible testing playbook and collect readiness evidence.
  5. Certification: evaluate the evidence and open the production gate only when readiness criteria are satisfied.
  6. Schedule: use Asset Inventory to align in-scope assets to the maintenance window defined by the application.
  7. App Notifications & Exceptions: notify application teams about their asset schedules and govern patch exemptions through linked Archer findings.
  8. Rollout & Deploy: execute the platform team's rollout and deployment playbook against the approved inventory scope with durable workflow continuity and safety controls.
  9. PIV: execute the platform team's post-implementation validation playbook and route failed validation into recovery.
  10. Track & Complete: reconcile completed, pending, failed and exempt assets for each environment; once every in-scope asset has a terminal result, mark the patch lifecycle completed, record the evidence package and return it to Patch Governance.

PATCH GOVERNANCE

Performance and assurance model

Patch Governance turns lifecycle events into a consistent operating view. It defines route eligibility, decision authority, risk-tier deadlines, escalation rules and the evidence required to close each item.

Core governance capabilities

  • KPI definitions for coverage, exposure, velocity, SLA health, first-pass success and evidence completeness
  • Risk burn-down and aging by severity, platform, application, environment and owner
  • Policy timelines, approaching-breach alerts and management escalation
  • Time-bound exceptions with approval, compensating-control and expiry evidence
  • Triage queues for blocked, overdue, deferred, exempt and failed-validation work
  • Executive, control-owner and audit-ready reporting from one lifecycle record

ASSURANCE

Evidence model

Evidence is created as work moves. The lifecycle record connects the original signal, accountable owner, decisions, approvals, execution status, timestamps and verified outcome.

What a review should answer

  • What risk entered the lifecycle, and when?
  • Which route was selected, under what rule, and by whom?
  • What execution occurred, including retries or failure recovery?
  • What result closed the work, and where is the supporting history?

CONFIGURATION MANAGEMENT

Configuration data model

The configuration domain builds a dependable reporting view by onboarding sources, aligning records to shared application context, assuring inventory and ownership, and preserving historical compliance results.

Stewardship responsibilities

  • Define and maintain each source's reporting contract.
  • Resolve ownership and application context.
  • Monitor completeness and changing requirements.
  • Expand platform coverage without fragmenting the control view.
