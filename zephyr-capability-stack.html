Intent goes down. Evidence comes back up.

Five tiers, top to bottom. A consumer declares what they need; the control plane turns it into governed, executable state; a provider runs it; someone keeps that provider running; a data centre carries all of it. Each tier holds capabilities the tier above should never have to think about.

Provider adapter boundary — one contract, many substrates
Tier 03

Cloud provider

The substrate's own control plane and managed services. Consumed through adapters, never directly. We orchestrate these capabilities; we do not rebuild them.

Value boundary — below this line you buy capability instead of building it
Tier 04

Provider-delivered capability

The real product a cloud provider sells sits here, not in the API. Keeping the platform patched, certified, staffed around the clock, audited and ahead of demand is an operating capability we would otherwise have to build. Buying it is the value. Where we run our own substrate, we produce that value ourselves — and should say so plainly.

Bought or produced, the bank still needs one answer. One patch posture, one capacity position, one incident view, one evidence package — assembled by the control plane regardless of who did the underlying work.

Physical boundary — where the stack stops being software
Tier 05

Data centre

Steel, power, cabling and silicon. For public cloud this tier is contracted and opaque. For RBC Cloud it is ours to plan, buy and run — which is exactly where the operating model differs.

The asymmetry that matters: for AWS and Azure, tiers 04 and 05 arrive as purchased capability — absorbed, priced into the bill, evidenced by a report. For RBC Cloud they arrive as headcount, change windows and a loading dock. That is not a weakness in the model; it is the cost line the provider's price tag is hiding, and it belongs in any comparison we publish.

Double click · compliance

One word, three different obligations

Compliance collapses three separate things into one label, and they sit at different tiers with different owners. Separating them is what makes the control plane's job definable.

Compliance of the substrate

Inherited. We evidence it; we do not run it.

  • SOC 1, SOC 2 and SOC 3 attestation reports
  • ISO 27001, 27017, 27018 certification scope
  • PCI DSS attestation and CSA STAR registry
  • Region, residency and sovereignty commitments
  • Sub-processor register and change notification
  • Penetration testing rules of engagement
  • Report access in place of a right to audit

On RBC Cloud there is nothing to inherit — we generate all of this ourselves.

Compliance in the substrate

Ours on every provider, without exception.

  • Encryption standards and key custody
  • Access, privileged access and joiner-mover-leaver
  • Logging completeness and retention periods
  • Segregation of duties across request and approval
  • Configuration baselines and drift closure
  • Vulnerability remediation within SLA
  • Resiliency, backup and DR testing evidence
  • Data classification and handling controls

Regulatory overlay

Bank-specific obligations that neither provider carries for us.

  • Third-party and outsourcing risk — materiality assessment
  • Concentration risk across providers and regions
  • Exit and portability plans that are actually tested
  • Technology and cyber risk management standards
  • Regulator right to examine, extended to the provider
  • Cross-border data movement and residency
  • Books, records and retention for Capital Markets
  • Operational resilience for the EU footprint
