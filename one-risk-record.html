02 — The symptom

One estate. One question. Five answers — and none of them wrong.

Ask "what percentage of the estate is patched to current baseline?" and every system answers honestly, from its own inventory, on its own refresh cadence, against its own definition of in-scope. The consolidated number is a blend of five different moments in time — a figure that never existed at any single instant.

03 — What it costs us

Seven ways a fragmented control environment bills us every day.

A — Data integrity

Every consumer creates another copy

  • Source → extract → landing → curated → dashboard. Five teams, four hops: roughly twenty materialisations of the same fact.
  • Silent pipeline failure produces silent divergence — no alarm, just a wrong number.
  • No shared as-of timestamp, so consolidation blends five refresh cadences.
  • Back-dated corrections rewrite one store and not the others; last month's report no longer reproduces.
  • No lineage — when a number is challenged, we can't show the path from asset to assertion.
B — Semantics

The same word means five things

  • "Compliant", "exception", "in scope", "critical" are defined per portal.
  • A 30-day critical clock in one domain is a 14-day clock in another.
  • No shared asset identity — hostname vs. serial vs. CI ID vs. IP. Consolidation runs on fuzzy matching and hand-maintained bridging tables.
  • Aggregation becomes arithmetic on incompatible units: directionally useful, forensically indefensible.
C — Coverage

Silent gaps look exactly like clean compliance

  • Each portal knows its own inventory. Nothing tells us about assets that appear in no portal.
  • Overlaps double-count: a database host is an asset in both Yoda and SPS, with two verdicts.
  • Domains with no portal at all — certificates, containers, third-party appliances — live in spreadsheets.
D — Control & governance

Exceptions live away from the record

  • Risk acceptances sit in portals with inconsistent approval trails and unenforced expiry — expired exceptions quietly persist as approved.
  • No aggregate residual-risk view, so prioritisation follows the loudest domain rather than the largest exposure.
  • We attest to a dashboard rather than to a controlled system of record.
E — Audit & regulatory

Irreproducibility is itself a finding

  • Lineage and reproducibility are examinable in their own right, independent of the underlying posture.
  • Same question, five evidence packs, five formats, assembled by hand each cycle.
  • Copies spread vulnerability and configuration data across more stores than needed — more surface, more access reviews, more retention obligation.
  • Retention and immutability differ per portal; some allow in-place edits with no history.
F — Cost & toil

Integration cost grows N×M, not N

  • Adding a sixth team is five new integrations, not one.
  • Permanent sustainment drag: pipeline monitoring, schema breakage, credential rotation, on-call for jobs with no 2 a.m. customer.
  • Five UIs, five auth models, five infra footprints, five patch and upgrade cycles — the compliance tooling is itself a compliance liability.
  • Reconciliation is a standing job. People spend longer explaining variance than reducing risk.
G — The human experience

Engineers enter the same fact five times, and leaders still ask the teams directly

  • The same evidence is keyed into several systems, in several formats, on several cadences.
  • Nobody fully trusts the consolidated view, so leadership goes back to the teams — recreating exactly the manual reporting the platform was built to remove.
  • Remediation lives in a different tool than the finding, so closing the loop is manual and closure evidence is an attachment.
  • Onboarding a team to central risk is a project, not a configuration.
04 — The trap

More pipelines cannot fix a problem caused by pipelines.

The instinct is to build better transport. But pipelines move copies, they don't reduce them — every fix adds a hop. And reliability multiplies: twenty pipelines at 99% each give roughly an 82% chance the enterprise view is correct on any given day. The divergence is semantic before it's technical, so perfectly reliable pipelines carrying incompatible definitions still produce the wrong answer — just faster.

Today: every source wired to every consumer Target: publish once against a shared contract

The fix is a contract and a single record — not more transport.

05 — The insight

The portals don't own the finding. They own the decision.

For most of these domains the platform portal isn't the origin of the data at all — it's a re-copy of an enterprise source, annotated with local decisions. Separating the two is what makes consolidation possible without taking anything away from anyone.

The finding

Redundant by definition

Originates from an authoritative enterprise source. Every portal copy of it is a duplicate that begins diverging the moment it lands. This is where twenty copies collapse to one.

The disposition

Valuable, and currently homeless

Accept, waive, remediate by date, false positive. Genuinely valuable, genuinely local — and with no enterprise system to live in, which is precisely why it ended up scattered across five portals.

We're not taking the portal away. We're giving disposition a real system — and the finding was never yours to store.

06 — By domain

Each control fails in its own characteristic way.

ITAM / Inventory

The denominator problem

  • Every other control is a fraction over inventory. If inventory is wrong, every compliance number is wrong by an unknown amount.
  • Five inventories that disagree: a host in SPS but not Yoda, an array decommissioned six months ago, a device CMDB never saw.
  • No canonical identity, which is why consolidation runs on fuzzy matching today.
  • Ghosts and orphans — and the orphans, the real risk, are the invisible ones.
  • Ownership drift: the named owner left, the app moved, the record didn't.
Patch levels

The moving-target problem

  • Compliance is time-relative — "current as of the July baseline" — and five portals refresh against five baselines.
  • Does an out-of-support OS count as unpatched, as EOL, or both? Teams answer differently, so hosts are double-counted or missed.
  • Remediation happens in one system and the record updates in another, days later — so teams keep a private spreadsheet of what's actually true.
  • Windows and waivers are invisible in the aggregate, so leadership sees red where an approved plan exists.
TSS scan results

The disposition problem

  • One origin, exported into every portal — and the copies start diverging the moment they're annotated.
  • False positives are closed locally and stay permanently open in the scanner. Audit sees the scanner.
  • Exception expiry isn't enforced consistently; lapsed acceptances persist as approved.
  • Closure evidence is a screenshot, not a re-scan.
  • Repeat findings aren't linked across cycles, so "new problem" and "same problem, ninth quarter" look identical.
IAM / entitlements

The reconciliation problem

  • Entitlement truth lives in the directory, recertification decisions in the portal — and the two agree only at campaign time.
  • Five different definitions of "privileged"; service accounts modelled least consistently of all.
  • Access granted for an incident or project rarely gets revoked — nothing ties entitlement to a lifecycle event.
  • One person's DBA rights, compute root and network admin sit in three portals, so aggregate blast radius is never seen.
  • Segregation-of-duties and toxic-combination analysis is structurally impossible today.
07 — Why now

The blocker wasn't the idea. It was that there was nowhere to put the data.

Portal databases are transactional stores sized for one team's current state. They physically cannot hold the estate's full observation history — which is exactly why every portal overwrites yesterday's answer with today's. That constraint is now gone. RBC Cloud runs a horizontally scalable, multi-region analytical warehouse built for high-volume, append-only, time-stamped observation data.

Scale

Billions of rows a year

Assets × domains × controls × daily observation. A columnar store absorbs it; a portal database cannot. This is the technical reason one store for the whole estate is now a real option.

History

Every observation, not just the latest

Point-in-time reproducibility, trend analysis, repeat findings linked across TSS cycles, drift over time. Reproducibility becomes a property of the storage model rather than a promise.

Retention

Multi-year evidence, cheaply

Compressed columnar storage ends the trade between audit history and storage cost — the trade every portal made silently.

Performance

Full-estate answers in seconds

The enterprise view is live, not a pre-computed nightly rollup that's stale before anyone opens it.

Resiliency & locality

Three zones, reads stay local

The platform survives a site loss, and residency obligations are met by placement rather than by standing up regional instances — which is how the copy problem returns geographically.

Growth

Add a shard, not a system

Onboarding domain six or team six becomes a capacity decision instead of an architecture decision. The scaling model matches the onboarding model.

The platform that measures resiliency, patch currency and TSS compliance must itself be resilient, current and compliant.

08 — The target

One Risk Record.

Zephyr holds the single authoritative compliance record for every asset in the estate. Platform teams keep their operational systems. They stop keeping compliance systems.

Stage 01

Measure at source

Collect directly from the server, array, device or subsystem — or accept a signed published feed where direct collection isn't practical. One hop, never four.

Stage 02

Land once, append only

Every observation carries a canonical asset ID, as-of time and observed-by. Nothing is overwritten; corrections are new observations.

Stage 03

Decide centrally

Disposition — accept, waive, remediate by date, false positive — with owner, rationale, expiry and automatic reversion, transactionally recorded against the finding.

Stage 04

Serve many lenses

One dataset, role-based views. The DBA sees the Yoda view, the network engineer sees the Networkview view. Teams lose the portal, not their view.

Move 1

One canonical model

A shared risk schema — asset, domain, control, assertion, evidence pointer, as-of, severity, SLA clock, exception, owner. Defined once, versioned, governed.

Move 2

One identity spine

Every assertion resolves to a canonical asset ID reconciled from CMDB and discovery. No assertion is accepted without one — which turns today's silent gaps into visible findings.

Move 3

Publish, don't pull

Teams publish against the contract, or Zephyr collects from source directly. Either way it's one hop and no bespoke per-team extraction.

Move 4

Two stores, one truth

The warehouse is the system of record for what was observed. A transactional layer is the system of record for what we decided. Neither pretends to be the other.

Named honestly: a warehouse alone is an observation layer. Disposition is workflow — approvals, state, expiry — and needs a transactional home alongside it. Saying so up front makes it a design choice rather than a gap someone else finds.

09 — Principles

Seven rules that keep it simple as it grows.

Measure once, use everywhere

A fact is captured at its origin and never re-derived.

Evidence, not assertion

Every verdict points to raw evidence that can be re-inspected.

Append, never overwrite

History is the product, not a byproduct.

Time is a first-class field

Every record carries as-of and observed-by. The enterprise view is a snapshot, not an average of five clocks.

Contract over pipeline

Schema changes are versioned and negotiated, not discovered when a job fails.

The gap is a finding

No data from a platform is a control failure, not a blank cell.

Exceptions are first-class

Risk acceptance lives with the record — owner, rationale, expiry, automatic reversion.

Reproducibility is a control

Any published number can be regenerated exactly, months later.

11 — The commitment

A warehouse is only a simplification if the portals actually retire.

If we stand this up and every portal stays, we haven't collapsed twenty copies to one — we've built copy twenty-one with better technology. The warehouse is the enabler; retirement is the outcome. Each portal gets a named decommission date in the plan, not a hoped-for second phase.

Retired as compliance systems

Yoda, SPS, TIC Portal, Networkview and the storage store. Where they remain useful as operational tooling they keep that role — they simply stop being where compliance truth is stored, curated and reported.

Retired outright

The bilateral pipelines, the bridging and mapping tables, the manual reconciliation cycle, and the per-portal evidence packs.

Kept and given a real home

Every disposition, waiver and recertification decision the teams have made — migrated into a system that enforces expiry and survives an audit.

12 — Getting there

Sequenced by domain, not by team.

Start with ITAM: it's the denominator under every other control, and nobody defends their asset list as strategic territory. Then TSS — highest scrutiny, clearest single origin, most obvious redundancy. Patch third. IAM last, because it's the hardest and needs the identity spine mature.

Phase 0

Agree the contract

Canonical schema, asset identity spine, shared definitions of compliant, exception, severity and SLA. No code. This is where the real work is.

Exit: signed data contract v1
Phase 1

Prove it on ITAM

Land inventory from CMDB and discovery into the warehouse, resolve canonical identity, and run in shadow beside the existing portals.

Exit: canonical asset spine live
Phase 2

Prove equivalence

Publish variance between Zephyr and each incumbent portal until it is small and fully explainable. This is what earns the right to retire anything.

Exit: variance explained and accepted
Phase 3

Cut over and retire

Zephyr becomes the reporting source. The portal goes read-only, then off. The first retirement is the proof point that makes teams four and five volunteer.

Exit: first portal decommissioned
Phase 4

Onboard by contract

TSS, then patch, then IAM. Each new domain is configuration against the contract, not a project.

Exit: four domains consolidated
Phase 5

Extend to the uncovered

Certificates, containers, third-party appliances — the domains that never got a portal and live in spreadsheets today.

Exit: no domain without a record

Natural first vehicle: the SQL Server pod. Same ninety-day shape, same governance, and Tigers are already in the room.

13 — What it's worth

Four currencies — and one of them isn't savings at all.

Confidence

One number, defensible

Reproducible months later, with lineage from asset to assertion, under examination.

Time

Reconciliation gone

Manual evidence assembly ends. Marginal cost of onboarding a domain drops from a project to a config.

Cost

Four portals removed

Their infrastructure, licences, upgrade cycles and patching go with them, along with pipeline sustainment.

Risk

The gaps become visible

Unmanaged estate surfaces, exception expiry is enforced, sensitive data sits in fewer places.

And the strongest argument isn't any of those. It's the set of controls that cannot be performed at all today:

ITAM

The unmanaged estate becomes visible for the first time, and every other control's denominator becomes defensible.

Patch

A single as-of enterprise position, with waivers and maintenance windows visible inside the same number.

TSS

Findings linked across cycles, exceptions that expire, closure proven by re-scan rather than by attachment.

IAM

A cross-platform view of a single identity — and segregation-of-duties analysis no individual portal can perform.

Cost savings get a polite nod. "This control cannot be performed at all today" gets a mandate.

14 — Answered in advance

The five things you're about to ask.

"You're taking away my system."
You keep your view and lose the obligation to run a portal, patch it, and answer audit twice. The lens stays; the maintenance goes.
"Central will be slower than my local database."
Operational tooling stays local. Only the compliance record centralises — and full-estate queries return faster than any current portal manages on its own slice.
"One store is a single point of failure."
Three nodes across three availability zones, multi-region. More resilient than any of the five portals it replaces, each of which genuinely is a single point of failure today.
"Our domain is special."
The contract is versioned and extensible. Domain specificity lives in domain attributes, not in a separate system.
"We tried a central risk warehouse before."
A warehouse on its own is another copy. This one comes with named retirement dates for the portals it replaces. That's the difference.

Five sources of truth, twenty copies, no shared identity, and a reconciliation habit. We can stop paying for all four.
